ResQ-NetSee what fails next, before it happens.ResQ-Net is a live map of how Bengaluru's power, water, hospitals and telecom depend on each other. When something breaks (a power cut, a flooded area, a pumping station that stops), ResQ-Net plays out hour by hour what fails next, alerts the people responsible before their facility fails, and shows how much damage acting early avoids.Team: Error 418Event: Build Beyond '26(BMSCE)Problem statement: City utilities such as hospitals, water pumping, emergency services and telecom usually run independently during disasters, so limited power gets used poorly and failures cascade. Build a decentralized system where an autonomous agent represents each utility. The agents negotiate in real time, through bids, priorities and concessions, over a fluctuating power supply. Every allocation must respect hard capacity limits, avoid inconsistent states, balance priority against fairness, and minimize total damage across a simulated multi-hour disaster scenario.Why ResQ-Net: every link has already failed in BengaluruEvery link in ResQ-Net's failure chain has already broken in real life. Each example below comes from a published report. Where a source is unclear, it says so.The five strongest numbersA 20-minute power cut becomes about 4 hours without water. Pumps take nearly an hour to restart, then about 3 more hours to refill the city's reservoirs. By then at least 20% of Bengaluru's areas are affected, according to BWSSB officials.About 110 power disruptions hit BWSSB pumping stations in a single three-month summer stretch.In September 2022, one flooded pumping station cut water to more than 50 areas for two days.One substation fire blacked out nearly 90% of central Bengaluru localities, including Vidhana Soudha.During the 2024 water crisis, Apollo Bannerghatta was buying 40–50 water tankers a day after its borewells dried up.The failure chains, link by linkFlood → water pumping (September 2022)Bengaluru's Cauvery water travels about 100 km through three pumping stages: TK Halli, Harohalli and Tataguni. That's one chain with no bypass.The TK Halli pumping station flooded with all pumps under 40 feet of water. BWSSB's chairperson called it the first time a pumping station was totally flooded.18 pumps and motors were damaged, costing about ₹3 crore. Standby units were borrowed from the other two stages, and the station was restored in 36 hours against a 7-day estimate.The flood rose 1.5 m above the flood level the retaining walls were designed for, so the design assumption itself failed.Rainfall that season was 162% above average.Flood → powerThe Kadubeesanahalli power station flooded.BESCOM also deliberately cuts power to flooded apartment basements to prevent short circuits.So some outages are safety shutdowns, not damage. ResQ-Net treats both the same way: the supply is gone for a number of hours.Power → water (this happens even without floods)2019: TK Halli had 14 power disruptions in about two months. Officials said a five-minute cut causes about a half-hour stoppage in pumping. A sudden stop also sends water surging backwards, which can burst pipes.October 2024: lightning and rain damaged transformers feeding TK Halli and Harohalli, and both stations lost power at about 11 PM.Undated incident (the article gives no date): an electrical failure at TK Halli and Tataguni stopped pumps for about 8.5 hours.It hit the stages supplying 140 and 320 million litres a day.Affected areas included Hebbal, RT Nagar, Rajajinagar, Yelahanka and Banaswadi.Power → hospitalsFebruary 2023: Bowring and Lady Curzon Hospital lost grid power for about 12 hours after a cable was cut.It ran on its generator, with diesel topped up twice.Some patients moved to Victoria and Vani Vilas hospitals.That diesel buffer is the countdown ResQ-Net shows on every hospital.The 220 kV substation fire: load had to be rerouted to a substation at Victoria Hospital. That rerouting is the redundancy ResQ-Net measures (a strong link).September 2022, Ballari (Karnataka, not Bengaluru): two ICU patients died during a power cut at VIMS. The opposition said backup power failed; the government disputed that, citing a 1.5-hour backup. The cause is disputed.Water → hospitals (2024 crisis)Vydehi Hospital in Whitefield needs 8–10 lakh litres a day. BWSSB supply to its area had stopped, and its borewells were barely coping.Tanker prices rose from ₹80 to ₹120 per kilolitre in six months.Apollo Bannerghatta was spending ₹30,000–35,000 a day on water.In March 2024, BWSSB cut Cauvery supply to bulk consumers, including hospitals, by up to 20%.Power → telecom → hospitalsNIMHANS and Sanjay Gandhi Institute of Trauma and Orthopaedics lost landlines for two days after power to a BSNL exchange was cut.Mobile coverage inside operating theatres and ICUs was poor, so staff used personal phones.The report places this in mid-July during the Covid period. The year isn't stated directly.Nobody owns the whole chainThese chains cross agency boundaries: BESCOM and KPTCL (power), BWSSB (water), BSNL (telecom) and the hospitals themselves. BWSSB has had to publicly urge the power utilities for uninterrupted supply, and a senior Urban Development official had to chair a meeting to coordinate them. Nobody owns the whole chain. That is the gap ResQ-Net fills.Notes on the evidenceWe found no public tool that maps these dependencies for Bengaluru. Data: substations, water facilities, hospitals, telecom sites and major roads from OpenStreetMap; the Cauvery pumping chain (TK Halli → Harohalli → Tataguni).Graph: utilities don't publish feeder maps, so links are inferred from location:substations below 220 kV draw power from the nearest 220 kV+ substation;Cauvery water runs in parallel stages I-V through the three pumping stations, each station on its own dedicated power line;each city water facility is fed by one stage and takes power from its nearest substation;hospitals take water from the nearest water facility and power from the nearest substation (two substations if 200+ beds);telecom sites take power from the nearest substation.Engine: a discrete-event simulation. Each facility is UP, on BACKUP (with a countdown: diesel, tank, battery) or DOWN. A facility needs every resource it depends on, from any one of its suppliers. Failures pass along links with real delays: pump restart after a power trip, reservoir refill, repairs. When supply returns at the same moment a backup runs out, supply wins.The engine exists twice: sim/engine.py (Python, tested) and web/src/engine.js (browser). node tools/parity_check.mjs checks they give identical results.Tech stackPartBuilt withWeb appReact 18, Vite 5Mapdeck.gl 9 (pins, links, tiles), Esri basemaps (keyless)ChartsRechartsSimulationJavaScript discrete-event engine in a Web WorkerOfflinePWA: manifest + service worker (app and viewed map tiles cached)Data pipelinePython, OSMnx 2, GeoPandas, ShapelyOptional serverFastAPI + UvicornUse the real Bengaluru dataThe app ships with real OpenStreetMap data pulled on 5 Nov 2026: 1,080 hospitals, 185 substations, 386 water facilities, 55 telecom sites and 7,390 road segments.
